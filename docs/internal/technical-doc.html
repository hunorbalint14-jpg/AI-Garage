Overview

scope · whole app

AI Garage is an AI‑powered, white-label SaaS for UK garages. It tracks MOT / service / VED dates and sends customer reminders across email / SMS / WhatsApp; runs the workshop from booking through digital health check, authorisation, job and invoice; recovers declined and due work; captures payments; and keeps the garage's accounting package (Xero, QuickBooks or Sage) in sync as an MTD digital link.

What it does

  • Vehicle & customer CRM — registration-keyed records, DVSA-enriched.
  • Reminder engine — MOT, service and road-tax reminders drafted by Claude and sent on a per-location schedule.
  • Bookings & jobs — bay-aware scheduling with Stripe Checkout pay-links.
  • Invoicing — issued in-app, optional Stripe pay-link, mirrored to Xero.
  • Campaigns — broadcast email / SMS written by staff, with opt-in AI assist (compose-first, §07); marketing consent enforced per channel.
  • Mid-job quotes (DVI) — mechanic records a short video of extra work found, customer approves or declines via a token-gated link.
  • Audit log — append-only trail of staff-sensitive actions (settings, GDPR, financial, integrations) visible to owners/admins.
  • Multi-branch organisations — the organisation is the tenant; customers are shared org-wide, work stays per branch.
  • Membership plans — recurring service plans (Stripe subscriptions) with member discounts + an included-service allowance.
  • Platform billing — the garage's own AI Garage subscription: Starter / Pro / Growth tiers, metered overage, and a lapse policy that never gates core trading.
  • Refunds — Stripe refunds recorded as credit notes, mirrored to the accounting package.
  • Stock & purchasing — parts catalogue, suppliers and purchase orders; AI parts suggestions with margin rules; per-technician labour time tracking.
  • Digital health checks (eVHC) — red/amber/green inspection with photos, turned into an approvable quote (§21).
  • Work authorisation & job profit — immutable signed artefacts, variation re-auth, per-job P&L (§21).
  • Revenue recovery — the deferred-work bank with follow-ups (§22) and evidence-backed tyre-care recommendations (§23).
  • Trade accounts — account customers, consolidated invoices, payments ledger, statements (§25).
  • Courtesy cars — loan diary with a hire-style damage map and photo history (§21).
  • Onboarding — prelive mode, self-serve activation, a migration toolkit with AI column mapping, and an AI-written garage mini-site (§24, §26).

03

Multi-tenancy model

subdomain · slug · RLS

The tenant is the organisation. Its subdomain slug is organizations.slug; the organisation owns one or more locations (branches). A customer registers once per organisation and is visible org-wide, while operational work stays per-branch. Staff pick an active branch, held in a cookie and re-checked on every request. Tenant isolation is enforced by Postgres RLS, not by application code.

Naming & routing

The root domain comes from NEXT_PUBLIC_ROOT_DOMAIN. In development it's localtest.me:3000 (which resolves any subdomain to 127.0.0.1 without DNS changes); in production it's the real apex.

  • apex — marketing site, sign-up
  • <org-slug>.apex — tenant portals (staff + customer), the public booking widget and the garage mini-site (§26)
  • admin.apex — the platform-operator console (tenant-less; §28)
  • Retired slugs redirect to the current one (org_slug_history)

Custom domains are parked: organizations.custom_domain exists but the resolver never routes it (docs/custom-domains.md).

Active branch

Staff pick a branch in the top-bar switcher (and, on phones, the "Jump to…" sheet); after login, multi-branch users land on /staff/select-branch. setActiveLocation() writes the active_location cookie, but the cookie is never trusted alone: getStaffContext() resolves it against the user's accessible branches every request, falling back to the org's primary location, then the first accessible one. Org owners, admins and accountants (and platform admins) can act in any branch; location-only staff are limited to the branches they hold a location_users row for. ctx.location is the active branch; ctx.organization is the tenant.

Tenant-scoped data access

Three Supabase clients, chosen per call site:

  • User-scoped server (createClient() from lib/supabase/server.ts) — server components and actions; RLS does the isolation.
  • Browser (lib/supabase/client.ts) — client components.
  • Service-role (createAdminClient()) — bypasses RLS. Used for the org lookup in staff-context.ts (the slug isn't sensitive; membership is verified independently), webhooks, cron, and server actions that have already enforced ownership in code. Many newer tables are read-only to authenticated for exactly this reason: every write goes through a server path that checks ownership, so there is no direct-API write route to forge (e.g. tyre_recommendations, held_comms, reminders).

RLS helpers live in a non-API-exposed private schema and are security definer + stable so the planner can hoist them: is_org_member (any role) · is_org_owner · is_org_admin (owner | admin) · is_org_finance (owner | admin | accountant) · is_org_staff (employed anywhere in the org) · is_location_member (branch staff + org owner | admin) · is_platform_admin. New tables must ship with policies using these, scoped to authenticated, with auth.uid() wrapped as (select auth.uid()). Customer-facing rows additionally allow auth.uid()-matched self reads.

Write policies need the full check A WITH CHECK that validates only the caller's branch membership isn't enough when a row also references a customer-global entity. wheel_service_events originally let any branch member attach their branch to another organisation's vehicle; fixed in #619 with private.vehicle_in_location_org(), and verified by simulating an unprivileged member in psql. Validate every foreign reference a writable policy accepts.
04

Authentication & sessions

supabase auth · webauthn · mfa

Authentication uses Supabase Auth: email and password, a magic link for customers, and WebAuthn passkeys for staff and customers. Org-level roles get a passkey step-up as MFA. Sessions are cookie-based, with a 12-hour absolute cap enforced server-side by GoTrue's [auth.sessions] timebox. JWTs are ES256 and verified locally with getClaims(), so reading the user doesn't cost a network hop to Auth.

Identity model

  • auth.users — Supabase-managed. One per real human.
  • org_users — joins a user to an organisation with role owner, admin or accountant. Owner and admin access cascades to every branch. Accountant is finance-only (§15).
  • location_users — joins a user to a single location with a role + permissions JSON. Used for per-location staff (e.g. a mechanic at one branch).
  • customers — non-staff users. Carry an optional user_id so a customer can sign in and use the customer portal.
  • webauthn_credentials — registered passkeys per user.
  • platform_admins — operator accounts for admin.<root> (or the PLATFORM_ADMIN_EMAILS allowlist).

Session lifecycle

  1. The login server action calls Supabase Auth, which sets the sb-* cookies. Staff go through /staff/select-branch, which lets multi-branch users pick one and sets the active_location cookie; single-branch users go straight in.
  2. For owners, admins and accountants the staff layout checks for a passkey step-up (the signed ai_mfa_verified cookie). By default it only nudges; with OWNER_MFA_ENFORCED=true it hard-blocks at /staff/mfa.
  3. GoTrue refuses to refresh a session older than 12 h (the server-authoritative cap). The proxy also stamps ai_session_started_at and signs out early past 12 h, redirecting to /staff/login?expired=1 (or returning 401 for /api/*). The stamp cookie must outlive the cap: when its maxAge equalled the cap, it deleted itself and users stayed logged in for days (#580).
  4. Sign-out is a server action that calls signOut() and clears the stamp.

Dedupe & safety

  • 30-day dedupe per (vehicle, reminder_type, channel) — prevents accidental repeat sends.
  • Per-location channels — settings JSON controls which channels run for which reminder type.
  • Hard 100-row limit per location per tick — cron stays well under the 60s Vercel hobby budget.
  • Bearer auth — every cron handler requires Authorization: Bearer ${CRON_SECRET} (compared with safeEqual); Vercel injects this from the project env when calling the configured cron path.
  • Prelive gate — a branch with locations.live_at IS NULL sends no unattended comms; each cron still works out what it would send and records it in held_comms for the Go-live screen (§24). The gate lives in the cron routes, not in the send helpers, so password resets and staff-initiated sends keep working.
  • First-run cap — in the 24 hours after a branch goes live, reminders are capped at locations.first_run_cap (default 25); over-cap items roll to the next run rather than being marked sent.
  • Cross-sender caps — the deferred-work and tyre-care paths read the reminders log (plus review requests and deferred follow-ups) so a customer never gets stacked nudges. Every automated send must write its reminders row: a silently failed insert defeats every cap that reads it.
  • Run history — handlers call recordCronRun() on every path, skips included. The Vercel-scheduled jobs and the four 09:00 platform passes are watched for staleness by the ar-cron-stale rule (§28). The per-branch via-tick jobs aren't, since a quiet day is normal for them.
  • Dispatch (#638) — the tick calls its children through lib/cron/dispatch.ts. cronBaseUrl() returns the public root domain (https://${NEXT_PUBLIC_ROOT_DOMAIN}, or CRON_BASE_URL if set). It is never request.url's origin: Vercel Cron invokes the tick on the *.vercel.app deployment URL, which Deployment Protection guards. dispatchCron() uses redirect: "manual" and counts only a JSON 2xx as success, so a protection redirect or an HTML 200 can't pass as a run.
Failure mode If a dispatch fails (a child returns non-2xx, redirects, answers HTML, or the request errors), the tick still advances next_run_at so it doesn't loop on the same failure. It counts the failure (daily passes included), records its own run as ok: false, and puts the first error in the run detail on /admin/health. Channel-level failures (e.g. Twilio rejects a number) are written to reminders.status='failed' with the error message.
Incident · June–September 2026 Before #638 the tick fanned out to new URL(request.url).origin. Every child request got Deployment Protection's 302 to vercel.com/sso-api, fetch followed it, and the login page answered 200. So the tick logged ran N, failed 0 while no child route ran at all, from at least June until 29 Sep 2026 (the last automated reminder had gone out on 6 June). It surfaced only when the daily passes were added to the stale watch (#627). Diagnose a repeat by comparing cron_runs for Vercel-direct jobs against tick children.

Bank reconciliation

Once a garage's Stripe balance pays out, payout.paid fires on the connected account and the handler posts a matching bank line to the accounting package, so the accountant gets a ready-to-match line against the real bank feed. The payout id is the reference; the date is payout.arrival_date; the line carries no tax (VAT was settled on the underlying invoices). The fees gap (gross customer payments minus net payout) accumulates for the accountant to resolve as a periodic "Stripe fees" expense.

Making Tax Digital — a digital link, not a filer

AI Garage deliberately does not become HMRC-recognised filing software (docs/mtd-readiness.md). It generates the garage's sales invoices, so it holds the primary digital record of supplies. The job is to make the hand-off to the package that files — API sync, or a CSV designed for import — complete, lossless and provably healthy, which VAT Notice 700/22 accepts as a digital link. Per-line vat_treatment, branch invoice prefixes, the books-health report and dead-link alerts all serve that. Never describe the product as "HMRC-recognised".

Platform billing additions

  • Growth overage — Growth-tier subscriptions carry a metered per-location quantity, kept equal to the real location count by the daily overage-reconcile pass.
  • Lapse policy — a five-state tenantBillingState machine drives persistent owner banners. The invariant: core trading is never billing-gated; only premium features lock (docs/billing-lapse-policy.md).
Idempotency contract Every accounting write is guarded by an "id already set" check before calling the provider API, and Stripe webhook events are claimed in stripe_webhook_events before processing. Replays from Stripe (or a manual retry) are safe; the fast path for an already-synced payment is two DB reads and no provider call.

Mid-job quotes (DVI)

video · token-gated · upsell

A mechanic working on a job who finds extra work records a short video, attaches line items + total, and sends the customer a token-gated link to approve or decline. Approved items get copied into job_items so the existing job → invoice → Stripe Connect path stays untouched.

Why it exists

  • Kills "I never agreed to that" disputes — the video + the approved quote are the audit trail.
  • Higher upsell conversion than a phone call — seeing the worn pad beats a verbal description.
  • Customer-friendly — token link works without an account; works on a bus stop on a phone.

Data model

  • quotes (quote_type = 'job') — lifecycle row (draft / pending / approved / declined / rebooked / expired / cancelled / approved_after_close), token hash, expiry, totals. Unified with standalone quotes since the Quote Management epic (#253); the legacy job_quotes table was dropped in Phase 10 (#252).
  • quote_items — frozen item snapshot — preserves what the customer agreed to even if products change later.
  • quotes_increment_view RPC — atomic view counter + first-view stamp.
  • Storage bucket job-quote-videos (private) — path format {location_id}/{job_id}/{quote_id}.{ext}, max 80 MB, MP4/MOV/WebM only.

Lifecycle

FIG 13·Aquote raise → customer decide → apply
HTTP / action privileged · service role email notification

Security posture

  • Token gating — 32 random bytes, base64url-encoded, only the sha256 hash is stored. Constant-time compare via crypto.timingSafeEqual. Same pattern as doc_shares.
  • Mint-once token — to resend, staff must cancel + recreate the quote. Matches the doc-shares posture and prevents accidental link leaks via "resend" buttons.
  • Atomic status transition — update … where status = 'pending' with a single predicate prevents two concurrent approvals from both inserting items.
  • Bucket privacy — no anon/authenticated policy on storage.objects for this bucket. All reads through 30-min service-role-minted signed URLs.
  • Job-state guard — if the job closed while the customer was deciding, the row flips to approved_after_close and staff are emailed to reopen or raise a separate job.
  • Cron expiry — /api/cron/quote-expiry sweeps stale pending rows hourly at :30.

Audit trail

Eight quote-specific audit actions are recorded: quote.create, quote.send, quote.cancel, quote.view (no actor — customer-side), quote.approve, quote.decline, quote.rebook (v2), quote.expire (cron-driven, no actor).

Payment posture Approval does not take payment. Items are added to the job and the existing job-complete → invoice → Stripe Connect pay-link path applies the final amount, because labour can overrun the quoted hours. Deposit-on-approve via Stripe Checkout is a deliberate v2 toggle.

Tyre-care recommendations

rules engine · review queue · #596

Each night the engine looks for vehicles that are genuinely due a rotation or an alignment check, and queues a recommendation for staff to review with its evidence attached. Nothing sends until a person approves it. Gated by the tyre_care flag, which stays off until the data-protection review of the marketing sends is done.

Engine (lib/tyre-care.ts, pure)

  • Mileage estimate — a weighted regression over dated odometer readings from MOT tests, jobs, tyre checks and imported history. Odometers only go up, so when two readings contradict each other the newer one wins. Implausible rates (over about 330 miles a day) are dropped. The estimate is shown on the vehicle page.
  • Rotation — the baseline is the later of the last rotation and the last fitment (wheel_service_events, tyre_checks). It fires every 6,000 miles, or on a front/rear tread gap of 1 mm or more on its own. A car without a confirmed standard vehicle_wheel_profile is flagged, and can't be sent until staff confirm it, because staggered and directional setups can't be rotated.
  • Alignment — three evidence rules: a left/right gap of 1.5 mm or more on one axle, an MOT advisory about uneven wear, or steering/suspension work done without an alignment afterwards. Evidence must be ≤180 days old (advisories ≤730) and must post-date the last alignment.
  • Balancing (12,000 miles) is too weak a signal to message a customer about, so it shows as a point-of-service prompt on the job card instead (customerContactable: false).
  • Cooldown — nothing is re-recommended until both 3 months and 3,000 miles have passed since the matching service. Confidence: high = measured evidence, low = mileage estimate only; the queue sorts high first.

Pipeline

  • /api/cron/tyre-care (tick task) evaluates the home-branch fleet and upserts tyre_recommendations. It is idempotent per vehicle and service, and an item whose evidence no longer holds moves to expired. Each run also writes a tyre_care_runs row with coverage counts.
  • Queue — /staff/tyre-care, tabs pending_review / approved_sent / converted / dismissed / expired. It flags an MOT due within 45 days (so the two can be bundled), a wheel setup that isn't confirmed yet, and the contact cap.
  • Approve & send is compose-first (§07). It starts from a plain template that states the evidence, and AI touches the text only through the assist menu. A channel without marketing consent can't be ticked, and every ticked channel must have a message: nothing is skipped silently.
  • Attribution — /book?tc=… pre-selects the service and marks the recommendation converted.
  • Results — /staff/tyre-care/results shows 90-day booked %, paid revenue (followed through the booking → job → invoice), dismissal rate with ranked reasons, unsubscribes, coverage, and conversion split by service × evidence. There is also a dashboard tile for revenue roles.

The hardening added private.vehicle_in_location_org() to every WITH CHECK. Any new table with both a vehicle FK and a location FK needs the same cross-check.

Trade accounts & consumer finance

account customers · statements · bumper

Fleet and trade customers buy on account (#504). There is no parallel billing system: an account is a flag and terms on customers, a consolidated invoice is an ordinary invoices row, and payments go through a small ledger.

Data model

  • customers.account_customer, payment_terms_days (default 30), credit_limit, consolidated_billing. organizations.credit_control_mode (warn by default).
  • Consolidated invoice — an invoices row with job_id null, plus invoice_jobs rows. job_id is unique there, so a job can never be billed twice. Month-end runs across the whole org (runMonthEnd).
  • Ledger — payments + payment_allocations, allocated oldest invoice first (lib/allocation.ts). invoices.amount_paid is the rollup; paid still means fully covered. Ledger RLS is select-only (financial pattern) and writes go through the admin client.

Surfaces

  • Credit control — checkCredit runs at createBooking and at quote → booking, and asks staff to confirm through a warning when the limit would be exceeded.
  • Statements — lib/statement.ts (pure): opening balance, running balance, and aging on outstanding amounts. A synthetic settlement credit covers invoices paid outside the ledger (Stripe, mark-paid). Print page at /staff/customers/[id]/statement, plus email.
  • Dunning groups account customers — one email per customer, with the stage taken from the oldest invoice and spaced by the cadence like any other chaser (§24).
  • Portal — /dashboard/account for the account holder, with pay-balance-by-card through Stripe on the connected account. lib/account-payments.ts → applyAccountPayment is the one applier used by both staff and Stripe.
Legal position Trade accounts are exempt trade credit only while no interest or charges are applied: see docs/trade-credit-position.md. Consumer finance is separate: finance_applications + finance_provider_configs, launched from /staff/finance through the Bumper integration (/api/finance/bumper). The lender, not the garage, carries the credit agreement.

Parts pricing & supplier ordering

Parts are products rows matched by (location_id, sku), priced from cost by an org markup rule, and ordered from factors through a connector seam, so a trade-API integration can replace the manual path supplier by supplier without any caller changing (epic #499).

Pricing & AI suggestion

The maths lives in lib/parts-pricing.ts (pure, ex-VAT). Invalid rules fall back to a default multiplier, so a price always resolves. Edited under Settings → Payments & quotes.
  • AI parts — lib/ai-parts.ts proposes the parts a job likely needs, with fitment caveats. matchProduct ties each suggestion to the branch catalogue (0.7 match threshold) and the caller prices it. A margin guardrail warns below the target.

Security & compliance

rls · gdpr · pecr · pci

Tenant isolation is enforced in the database, with RLS helpers keyed to the org and the branch. PCI is offloaded to Stripe Checkout, so card data never reaches us. The platform records DPA acceptance per organisation, honours erasure with an auditable log, and treats marketing consent and opt-out as first-class data.

Application-level

  • RLS on every tenant table, through the private-schema helpers (§03): is_org_staff for customer-global data, is_location_member for operational data, and is_org_finance alongside branch membership for financial rows. New tables ship with policies scoped to authenticated, with (select auth.uid()) wrapped.
  • RLS audit (#458) — each role was simulated in psql against every table. It found and fixed anonymous exposure on organizations. The #619 hardening added a vehicle → org cross-check to the tyre tables' WITH CHECK clauses.
  • Deny-all tables — doc_shares, page_views, accounting_connections and supplier_integrations have no client policies at all, and are reached only through the service role.
  • Service-role key only in lib/supabase/admin.ts. It's used by webhooks, cron, token-gated public routes, the operator portal, and staff actions after an explicit permission check.
  • 12-hour absolute session — enforced server-side by GoTrue [auth.sessions] timebox = "12h". The ai_session_started_at cookie in updateSession() gives a friendlier early sign-out. Prod needs supabase config push (or the Dashboard setting), because the [auth] config isn't a migration.
  • MFA — a passkey step-up for owners, admins and accountants (lib/mfa.ts). It nudges by default and hard-blocks with OWNER_MFA_ENFORCED=true. Passkeys are available to staff and customers, with the root domain as RP id.
  • Signed webhooks — Stripe, Resend (svix) and Twilio are verified; a failed signature returns 400 with no side effects. Stripe events are claimed once in stripe_webhook_events.
  • Cron auth — the CRON_SECRET bearer is compared with safeEqual (constant time).
  • Token-gated links — quotes (q-/sq-), eVHC reports (hc-), re-authorisation (wa-), plan invites, reviews, deferred and tyre-care booking links, unsubscribe and doc shares. Only sha256(token) is stored, and comparisons are constant time.
  • Encrypted credentials — accounting OAuth tokens and supplier credentials are AES-256-GCM encrypted (lib/encryption.ts, APP_ENCRYPTION_KEY) and write-only from the UI.
  • Rate limiting — Upstash Redis throttles login, reset, magic-link, passkey and set-session. It fails open when unconfigured, so it must be set in prod.

Compliance hooks

  • DPA acceptance — organizations.dpa_accepted_at · dpa_version · dpa_accepted_by_user_id, enforced through the /staff/dpa-acceptance redirect.
  • Marketing consent — customers.marketing_email_consent · marketing_sms_consent · consent_updated_at, checked by campaigns, win-back and tyre care. Channels without consent can't be selected.
  • PECR opt-out — every marketing email carries a per-message unsubscribe token and RFC 8058 one-click headers, and SMS carries an opt-out link (§24).
  • Deliverability — hard bounces and complaints are suppressed before send (email_suppressions).
  • Right to erasure — anonymisation sets customers.anonymized_at, and data_deletion_log records the reason, the requester and an email hash. Anonymised customers are excluded from every automated sender.
  • Legal artefacts — work authorisations and courtesy-car agreements are immutable snapshots with a pinned wording version (§21).
  • Audit log — append-only audit_log, written by lib/audit.ts for staff mutations (settings, GDPR ops, money, integrations, quotes, authorisations, go-live, flag toggles) with actor, IP, user agent and JSON metadata.
  • First-party analytics — daily-rotating hashes and no cookies, so no consent banner is needed for traffic stats (§28). Non-essential cookies elsewhere stay behind the cookie banner.
  • MTD positioning — AI Garage is a digital-link source for bridging software, not an HMRC filer. Never describe it as "HMRC-recognised" (§09).
  • Data retention — organizations.data_retention_years. page_views are purged after 90 days and held_comms after 7 days.
